## Phase 2: Strong vs. Weak Scaling

**Setup:** The pipeline has two stages. Stage A runs serially in the parent process (first 5% of elements, 10 trig/sqrt operations each). Stage B runs in parallel on a persistent `multiprocessing.Pool` (30 FMA/trig operations per element). The Pool was created once, outside the timed loop. I tested 1 to 12 processes with 2 warmup runs and 7 timed runs. Strong scaling used N = 1,200,000 in total. Weak scaling used 200,000 elements per worker.

- Time went down as I added processes. Speedup was 1.94x at 2 processes and 11.94x at 12.
- Efficiency was above 100% at p = 3 to 11, which shouldn't happen in normal strong scaling. The IQR at p = 2, 3 and 4 was also very high. So I don't fully trust those results. Noise from the system is the likely cause.
- **Amdahl's Law:** serial fraction s = 96.6972 / 3953.1667 = 0.024461, so the maximum possible speedup is 1/s = 40.88. My 11.94 is below that, as expected, because of scheduling and communication overhead.

### p = 8 to p = 9

| Configuration | Speedup |
|---|---:|
| p = 7 | 10.7384 |
| p = 8 | 10.7329 |
| p = 9 | 11.5990 |

I expected speedup to flatten after 8 processes, since the CPU has 8 physical cores. It didn't. Going from 7 to 8 gave no gain (about 0.9995x), and going from 8 to 9 actually gained about 8% (1.0807x). This doesn't prove a ninth process always helps. The OS decides where processes run, and I didn't pin workers to specific cores. The i5-13420H is also a hybrid CPU, so process count doesn't map neatly to physical cores.

### How reliable are these results?

Several things make me cautious: high IQR at p = 2 to 4, efficiency above 100%, the jump in weak-scaling time at p = 11 and 12, and uneven Stage A timings. Background activity, CPU scheduling and power management could all be involved. These numbers describe this one run on this machine, not general hardware behavior.

## Conclusion

- Parallelism helped. Strong scaling reached 11.94x at 12 processes (Amdahl's cap: 40.88x). Phase 1 peaked at about 3.57x.
- Weak scaling looked good in theory (Gustafson 11.11x at p = 12), but real efficiency fell a lot as the workload grew.
- Serial work, process count, CPU design and system overhead all affect performance. Noisy measurements mean the results should be read with care.
